What we publish

Critical Quest is a mini publication venture that makes the best and most relevant social science writing from India and abroad available to students, teachers and the general reading public. It also works to retrieve the rational-liberative strands of Indian history and culture and hold them inside present-day debate.

The aim is not only to inform and enlighten, but to move readers towards an emancipatory engagement with the society around them — a tool for the deconstruction as well as the reconstruction of socially relevant knowledge.

Nothing here is profit-oriented. We invite the co-operation of everyone committed to the socio-political transformation of Indian societies towards greater social inclusion and more egalitarian practice.

How the books work

Each title is a single essay or lecture, set in 32 to 96 pages and priced between ₹25 and ₹60 — cheap enough to buy a dozen, short enough to finish in a sitting, and durable enough to teach from.

Authors range from Ambedkar, Phule, Periyar and Iyothee Thassar to Uma Chakravarti, Romila Thapar, Amartya Sen, Nancy Fraser, Althusser and Horkheimer.

      Who we are

      About

      Critical Quest is not a commercial venture. It is a self-financed publication effort, run by Josna T. Jacob (proprietor and manager) and edited by Gnana Aloysius.

      The two spent more than fifteen years as socio-political activists among the tribals of Jharkhand. Alongside that work they retrieved, recorded and published the fast-vanishing traditions of tribal assertion for collective identity and autonomy — a parallel effort to the political struggles themselves, and one that measurably strengthened solidarity and confidence.

      That experience is what this list broadens. The conviction behind it: for the gains of the economic and political struggles of India's subalternised social groups to hold, those struggles have to be rooted in their own earlier rational-liberative traditions — traditions that mainstream history has largely written out.

      So the catalogue runs in two directions at once. It brings in critical theory, social exclusion research and comparative political thought from elsewhere; and it puts Phule, Ambedkar, Periyar, Iyothee Thassar and Pandita Ramabai back into circulation as thinkers, not icons.
